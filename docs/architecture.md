# Architecture

Detailed notes on the model and the renderer, moved out of `CLAUDE.md` so they load only when needed. Times are in simulated units of 2.5 years unless given in years.

## meander-core (`src/`)

### Step

`Sim::step` resamples the centreline and migrates it with the *Howard–Knutson* kernel. Its local term reads the curvature through a [1, 6, 1] filter, since at `dt` a zigzag from point to point otherwise overshoots and flips direction each step. The filter halves the term's hold on kinks, so `smoothing` is 0.045, up from 0.03, which keeps the seed fuzzer free of self-crossings. It then smooths and clamps it and runs the neck cutoff and any capture, resampling after every cut. A point where the line turns back by more than 120° is a hairpin too short for the neck cutoff, and smoothing moves it to the middle of its neighbours. The rate is scaled by channel slope (`Sim::slope`): the valley slope over the sinuosity within `SLOPE_REACH`, raised to `slope_exponent`, relative to `slope_sinuosity`. Winding reaches migrate more slowly, so necks close later. Migration reads the grid and the wall taper at the bank a point moves into, chosen by the sign of the rate. A recent cut does not hold back a neck whose banks have closed to half the cutoff distance. A random sideways push, `jitter`, keeps the river from straightening as bends drift out of the domain. Each point reads it from value noise over the floodplain, redrawn every `jitter_time` (5 years) and blended between draws, so a push turns gradually and does not flip from step to step. `JITTER_BLEND` scales it to the long-run drift of a fresh push each step.

### Erodibility and vegetation (`field.rs`)

Erodibility is *fractional Brownian motion* (fBm) noise on the grid. Each step stamps the cells under the channel as bare point-bar sand. Growth binds sand as shrub at `vegetation_time` (250 years) and stiffens it to old-floodplain erodibility as forest after a further `forest_time`. It advances one of 10 bands of rows per step, faster on deeper overbank deposit up to `vegetation_height`. Overbank silt is laid at `overbank_rate`, one of `DEPOSIT_EVERY` (50) bands per step. It thins away from the bank over `levee_length`, stops at `levee_height`, and slows erosion on high banks.

### Valley walls (`walls.rs`)

The walls are set at each reset from a seed stream of their own. Each foot wanders outwards of `valley_width`, and *combes* cut back into it. `Sim::floor` gives the floor; the taper reads it narrowed at the pinches, the land survey without them. Banks pressing on a wall retreat its foot (`Walls::erode`), and each half width of retreat slumps colluvium and bumps `wall_version`.

### Oxbows, scroll bars and width

A cutoff stamps an *oxbow* lake. It fills over `oxbow_lifetime` to a clay plug, faster under overbank silt or with bedload at `bedload_rate`, then stays as a scar for `scar_lifetime`. The channel reworks oxbow within `REWORK_RADIUS`, and a new oxbow absorbs older ones under its water. `Sim::capture` takes up an open lake (fill below `CAPTURE_FILL`) that meets the channel twice, when the bypassed channel is longer than the lake route by more than `capture_ratio`. `Sim::chute` cuts a *chute* across the point bar of a bend at least `CHUTE_RATIO` (3) times as long as a chord of 1.5–5 widths. It scans `CHUTE_SCAN` (8) points a step, and cuts with chance `chute_rate` times the supply and the share of the chord that is bare bar, so vegetation binding a bar stops it. The neck search and the rework share one hashed grid, rebuilt only when `line_version` changes. The neck search builds it with the capture search's larger cells, which serve both, so a step builds the grid twice, down from 2.9 times. The median native step fell from 0.111 to 0.107 ms (seed 7, 20,000 steps), with the same output. `oxbow_version` counts shape changes, not fills, so `sync` can skip recomputing heights.

*Scroll bars* come every `scroll_time` (125 years) on average along the inner banks of migrating bends, raised by `scroll_height`. Local width (`Sim::widths`) follows the bank-pull and bar-push model of Parker et al. (2011), through `bar_push` and `width_time`. It changes the planform only through the bank reads and the cutoffs.

### Landholdings (`land.rs`)

Holdings are the *geodesic Voronoi cells* of farmsteads, with the channel and oxbows as barriers and flood risk as the cost of crossing a cell. Risk falls with distance from water, and on high deposit by up to `RIDGE_RELIEF`. *Wet ground* is reworked ground that has lain low for over 250 years, its deposit under `WET_DEPTH` (0.7) of the flood level, at least 2 widths from water and off the oxbow scars. Never-swept ground stays dry, since it stands as old floodplain. Wet ground carries a risk of at least `WET_RISK` (0.6), so farmsteads leave it and its fields are meadow. Holdings take up ground once grassed for `grazing_time` (50 years). One at a time, every `reorganise_time` (150 years) on average, a holding moves a threatened seat, is absorbed below 0.3 `holding_area`, takes cheaper cells, or divides above twice `holding_area`. At `BOUNDARY_TIME` (1,000 years) the centreline is kept as a *parish boundary*, which stays put as the channel moves on. Crossing it costs a holding `BOUNDARY_COST` (8 widths) more, so fields settle on it wherever a holding on its far side can reach the ground. Holdings change the river only through their mills.

### Farmsteads (`farm.rs`)

Each reorganisation changes a farmstead by one building, towards a plan set by the seat's risk against `SEAT_RISK`. A building past `FLEE_RISK` or off the holding is abandoned first, and ruins fade over `RUIN_TIME` (150 years). Buildings keep `CLEAR` of tributaries, whose courses `Sim` hands over when they change. Each survey removes buildings the water has reached, and queues a new house for any holding left without one, so it rebuilds within 10 years instead of waiting for its next reorganisation. A division makes a hamlet with chance `hamlet_chance`. The farmsteads draw from their own *pseudo-random number generator* (PRNG) stream and spend none of the holdings' search budget, so with `hamlet_chance` 0 the holdings and the river run exactly as with `farmsteads` off.

### Tracks and fords (`tracks.rs`)

Tracks join the farmsteads and cross the channel only at fords, since no bridge would stand for long. One search goes out from every farmstead at once, `TRACK_BUDGET` (128) cells a step, costed by flood risk and by `FORD_COST` for each width of channel waded. Its parts meet in 8 bands of rows, one a step. The cheapest meetings are laid first, as a *greedy spanner*: a lane goes in only if the network already laid makes the trip more than `DETOUR` (2) times as long. Ground on the last network costs `TRACK_KEEP` (half), so ways stay in use. A network takes about 340 steps (85 years). The fords are found again on the tracks after every survey of the channel, so they follow the water. Tracks read the land and change nothing else.

### Floodplain processes

- Clearance: `Sim::supply()` rises from 1 to `clearance_supply` from `clearance_start` (1,500 years) over `clearance_time` (1,000 years). It multiplies overbank silt, lake filling and fan growth.
- Wood (`wood.rs`): mature forest on eroding banks drops jams. A jam pushes the flow off its bank and breaks up over `jam_lifetime`, or is lost beyond `JAM_HOLD` of the centreline.
- Tributaries (`tributary.rs`): combes 3 widths deep or more carry a stream. It builds a gravel fan into the `coarse` grid (`coarse_factor`) and speeds migration below its confluence. Its course is re-cut every 10 steps at the first water, so it changes only at its lower end. It shifts on its fan at `stream_shift_rate` (once per 1,000 years) and builds a delta into an open lake at `delta_rate`.
- Mills (`mills.rs`): holdings found mills on straight reaches. A *weir* holds its reach and ponds the reach above until the channel outflanks or leaves it. A *leat*'s ends follow the bank, and the mill is ruined when the bank reaches its course.

### Hydraulics and PRNG streams

`ips.rs` derives Ω, Γ and α from hydraulic inputs, and `rng.rs` is the local seeded PRNG. Falling wood, new mills and scroll bars draw from `events`, tributary shifts from `streams`, and walls and farmsteads from streams of their own. Turning a process off leaves the river's own draws unchanged.

## meander-wasm (`wasm/`)

`River` wraps `Sim` with hidden reaches of `UPSTREAM` = 60 and `DOWNSTREAM` = 20 widths. `sync()` copies geometry into flat `f32` buffers exposed as raw pointers. `sync_scrolls`, `sync_plugs` (time since cutoff per oxbow cell) and `sync_forest` (growth per cell) can also grow WebAssembly (WASM) memory, so the worker's `pack` calls them all before it reads the memory's buffer. `sync_line` copies only the centreline and widths, for the warm-up's point-bar capture. `soil_ptr` points at the erodibility noise, fixed at reset, and `param_names` lists the parameters. `jams`, `buildings`, `tributaries` and `mills` return `Vec<f32>` copies, which also allocate. `coarse_ptr` points at the gravel grid, and `mill_version`, `building_version` and `wall_version` tell the renderer when to rebuild.

## web (`web/`)

The river steps in a *dedicated worker* (`worker.js`), a thread of its own, while the page draws. After each request the worker packs everything the renderer reads into one `ArrayBuffer` and transfers it without copying (`pack` in `snapshot.js`). On the page, `RiverView` answers the same calls as `River` from that snapshot. Each `*_ptr` is a byte offset into the snapshot's buffer, and `memory.buffer` is the buffer itself, so the renderer builds its views as it would on WASM memory. A renderer read of a new `River` accessor needs an entry in `snapshot.js`. `soil` reads the noise grid sent at each reset, and `wall_foot` reads samples of the walls' feet along the window, sent when `wall_version` changes. A snapshot is 1.2 MB for a desktop window 768 units wide and 1.4 MB for a phone's 1,039, mostly seven field grids. Packing one takes 0.2 ms in Node (seed 7, after 1,000 units).

`ui.js` owns the panel, the frame loop and the warm-up. It keeps one stepping request in flight and draws the latest snapshot. A reset drops the replies to earlier requests. The warm-up runs in requests of `WARM_BUDGET` (12 ms), and the worker returns the centreline after every 50 steps for the point bars. The panel shows the speed slider, the tick (`River::steps` since the reset, warm-up included) and JavaScript frame-time percentiles.

A replay restarts the river, runs it to a tick and pauses. The Link's `view=W` is the window width in world units, because the river's bounds follow the window and a slightly wider one soon diverges. The warm-up and replays record the centreline every 50 steps from the reset, so two replays of one link give identical Scalable Vector Graphics (SVG). A live run records each frame, so its point bars differ. `ui.js` sets `data-idle` once a paused page has nothing left to draw. The loop draws only after a step or a setting change, and schedules no frames while paused with no floodplain redraw under way. The channel is the exception: while the river runs, it trails the river by one step, and `tween` redraws it between the last two centrelines. Each point of the newer line moves out from its foot on the older one, and a cutoff makes the channel jump across. At the default speed the river steps every third frame by up to a pixel, so the channel would otherwise move at 20 Hz; trailing it costs about 1.2 ms a frame. The feet are found once a pair, with the furthest any point or bank in the window moves between the two. A frame redraws the channel only once it could have moved `MIN_MOVE` (0.5) device pixels since the last redraw. At the default speed it moves 0.1–0.3 CSS pixels a frame on an 844 × 390 phone, so on a phone with 3 device pixels to the CSS pixel 177 of 301 frames redraw it. At 8× throttling there, the mean frame fell from 14.1–14.2 ms to 8.5–8.9 ms over three alternating 10 s runs. The device pixel scale is read once per resize, since reading the window's size after the frame's writes forced a layout, which raised `tween` at speed 60 from 9.4 to 12.8 ms a frame.

`main.js` draws three stacked SVGs: `#bars` (old point bars), `#ground` (floodplain) and `#view` (channel and lakes). The first two are *compositing layers*, so a moving channel does not repaint them. Floodplain layers share `FLOODPLAIN_BUDGET` (4 ms) a frame, or `FLOODPLAIN_IDLE_BUDGET` (10 ms) on an otherwise idle frame, oldest picture first. When a redraw has been due for longer than the lag limit, `ui.js` holds the river until the floodplain catches up. The limit is `MAX_LAG` (25 years) or `LAG_SECONDS` (0.5 s) at the set speed, whichever is longer. With the limit in simulated time alone, a redraw spread over 10 frames at full speed held the river twice a second. Before the hold, the river slows to the speed at which every redraw under way would finish inside the limit, from a running mean of each layer's redraw time in real time, scaled by 1 / `PACE_LAG` (1). A margin of 1 / 0.7 slowed the river by 1–11% on a phone at 4× throttling, and neither stood still for 3 frames. A redraw's time counts at most `REDRAW_GAP` (100 ms) a frame, so a redraw due while the tab was hidden does not slow the river after it. A redraw takes about as long in real time at any speed, so on a slow machine the river settles at a steady lower speed. It slows at once and speeds up over `PACE_SECONDS` (1 s), though a new speed from the slider takes effect at once while the floodplain is not holding the river back. The floodplain's budget rises towards the idle budget only as a redraw passes half the lag. Giving it the idle budget whenever the floodplain set the speed ran the river 17% faster on the 8× phone at speed 60 (12.5 against 10.7 units/s), but cut the frames that showed it moving from 28 to 24 a second. A redraw *episode* ends when its layer shows a new picture, even if the next is due at once; counting from the first due frame instead let the lag grow across back-to-back redraws. On Chrome at 8× throttling on an 844 × 390 viewport, seed 7 from tick 10,000 at speed 60, the river held in 251 of 377 frames over 10 s with the hold alone. With the pacing it moved in every one of 256 frames, at 13.6 units a second (34 years a second). A desktop at speed 60 steps in every frame, at 58 units a second. A stepping request may take `STEP_SHARE` (80%) of the last frame's interval, at least `STEP_BUDGET` (8 ms), and `ui.js` drops the simulated time left over, and any owed beyond the request under way, one step and `MAX_OWED` (40 ms at the river's speed) while a reply is late, so the river does not lurch on when it comes. Without that, a machine too slow for the set speed owes each frame the steps of the last, longer one: on a phone-class CPU (Chrome at 8× throttling) at full speed, frames reached 200 ms when the river stepped on the page's thread. Lake rebuilds share `LAKE_BUDGET` (2 ms) a draw, in turn, because at full speed every lake's fill passes a `LAKE_STEP` between draws. A lake the channel has reshaped rebuilds at once. On a pause or a single step, `settle` finishes every redraw in one frame, and the draw after it rebuilds every waiting lake.

### Channel and lakes

The channel is a filled outline at the local width. Only points within `CHANNEL_MARGIN` of the window are drawn, though curvature and widths span the whole river. Pools, riffles and mid-channel bars come from the smoothed curvature and width; the riffles and the bars change slowly, so each redraws one draw in three. A lake is drawn on its bed, a parabola across the old channel (`lakeBed`). Reeds fill the water shallower than the open water's edge, so they reach furthest over a gently sloping bed and grow out over each plug. Every lake's reeds lie under the water of all lakes, so lakes that meet read as one. Over the water lie two deeper bands, set towards the old outer bank by the bend's curvature; silt plumes that fade along the lake from each end, and around the head of a delta, stronger as clearance raises the supply; water lilies on the shallows from about 750 years after the cutoff; and duckweed mats late in the fill. Lilies and duckweed are seeded by their place along the whole oxbow, as the scar's tufts are. `tributaries` marks a stream that ends in a lake, for its delta's plume.

### Floodplain

Fields are strips along each holding's long axis. They redraw after a reorganisation, at most every `FIELD_REFRESH`, and write the page only when done, swapping in twins of the paths that changed. Contours fade them in from `grazing_time` to `vegetation_time`, so they follow the channel onto new bars. Forest growth is contoured into shrub and forest, a contour a stage, and sizes the tree crowns, a `TREE_STRIP` at a time; a crop mask clears crowns from fields. Each stage sets its path data on detached twins of the paths, since Chrome parses path data when it is set, and the last stage swaps the twins onto the page. A finished forest redraw drops any crown redraw under way, which would use stale growth. Farmsteads lie under the crowns and redraw on `building_version`, at least every 50 years. Wet ground is blurred twice and contoured into marsh with the survey maps' reed sign, every `COARSE_REFRESH` (50 years). Tracks lie under the farmsteads and redraw when they change; a cell that several tracks share is drawn once. The parish boundary is drawn once, as a dash-dot line over the crowns and under the water.

### Valley walls, streams, jams and mills

The walls are drawn from `wall_foot` and `wall_combes` without the pinches, which only keep bends off the window edges. They redraw after a slump, at most every 50 years, in budgeted stages, and the page and the wall lines the field mask reads change only in the last stage. Each scarp is shaded in nested bands, darker on the top wall, which faces away from the upper-left light, and lighter on the bottom wall. Its hachures are wedges that thin down the slope, as on old survey maps. Tributaries redraw when a course changes. Leats lie under the water, and fords, jams and mills over it. A working weir follows the channel, and its leat redraws each frame to the bank.
