# Architecture

Detailed notes on the model and the renderer, moved out of `CLAUDE.md` so they load only when needed. Times are in simulated units of 2.5 years unless given in years.

## meander-core (`src/`)

### Step

`Sim::step` resamples the centreline and migrates it with the *Howard–Knutson* kernel. Its local term reads the curvature through a [1, 6, 1] filter, since at `dt` a zigzag from point to point otherwise overshoots and flips direction each step. The filter halves the term's hold on kinks, so `smoothing` is 0.045, up from 0.03, which keeps the seed fuzzer free of self-crossings. It then smooths and clamps it and runs the neck cutoff and any capture, resampling after every cut. A point where the line turns back by more than 120° is a hairpin too short for the neck cutoff, and smoothing moves it to the middle of its neighbours. The rate is scaled by channel slope (`Sim::slope`): the valley slope over the sinuosity within `SLOPE_REACH`, raised to `slope_exponent`, relative to `slope_sinuosity`. Winding reaches migrate more slowly, so necks close later. Migration reads the grid and the wall taper at the bank a point moves into, chosen by the sign of the rate. A recent cut does not hold back a neck whose banks have closed to half the cutoff distance. A random sideways push, `jitter`, keeps the river from straightening as bends drift out of the domain. Each point reads it from value noise over the floodplain, redrawn every `jitter_time` (5 years) and blended between draws, so a push turns gradually and does not flip from step to step. `JITTER_BLEND` scales it to the long-run drift of a fresh push each step.

### Erodibility and vegetation (`field.rs`)

Erodibility is *fractional Brownian motion* (fBm) noise on the grid. Each step stamps the cells under the channel as bare point-bar sand. Growth binds sand as shrub at `vegetation_time` (250 years) and stiffens it to old-floodplain erodibility as forest after a further `forest_time`. It advances one of 10 bands of rows per step, faster on deeper overbank deposit up to `vegetation_height`. Overbank silt is laid at `overbank_rate`, one of `DEPOSIT_EVERY` (50) bands per step. It thins away from the bank over `levee_length`, stops at `levee_height`, and slows erosion on high banks.

### Valley walls (`walls.rs`)

The walls are set at each reset from a seed stream of their own. Each foot wanders outwards of `valley_width`, and *combes* cut back into it. `Sim::floor` gives the floor; the taper reads it narrowed at the pinches, the land survey without them. Banks pressing on a wall retreat its foot (`Walls::erode`), and each half width of retreat slumps colluvium and bumps `wall_version`.

### Oxbows, scroll bars and width

A cutoff stamps an *oxbow* lake. It fills over `oxbow_lifetime` to a clay plug, faster under overbank silt or with bedload at `bedload_rate`, then stays as a scar for `scar_lifetime`. The channel reworks oxbow within `REWORK_RADIUS`, and a new oxbow absorbs older ones under its water. `Sim::capture` takes up an open lake (fill below `CAPTURE_FILL`) that meets the channel twice, when the bypassed channel is longer than the lake route by more than `capture_ratio`. `Sim::chute` cuts a *chute* across the point bar of a bend at least `CHUTE_RATIO` (3) times as long as a chord of 1.5–5 widths. It scans `CHUTE_SCAN` (8) points a step, and cuts with chance `chute_rate` times the supply and the share of the chord that is bare bar, so vegetation binding a bar stops it. The neck search and the rework share one hashed grid, rebuilt only when `line_version` changes. The neck search builds it with the capture search's larger cells, which serve both, so a step builds the grid twice, down from 2.9 times. The median native step fell from 0.111 to 0.107 ms (seed 7, 20,000 steps), with the same output. `oxbow_version` counts shape changes, not fills, so `sync` can skip recomputing heights.

*Scroll bars* come every `scroll_time` (125 years) on average along the inner banks of migrating bends, raised by `scroll_height`. Local width (`Sim::widths`) follows the bank-pull and bar-push model of Parker et al. (2011), through `bar_push` and `width_time`. It changes the planform only through the bank reads and the cutoffs.

### Landholdings (`land.rs`)

Holdings are the *geodesic Voronoi cells* of farmsteads, with the channel and oxbows as barriers and flood risk as the cost of crossing a cell. Risk falls with distance from water, and on high deposit by up to `RIDGE_RELIEF`. *Wet ground* is reworked ground that has lain low for over 250 years, its deposit under `WET_DEPTH` (0.7) of the flood level, at least 2 widths from water and off the oxbow scars. Never-swept ground stays dry, since it stands as old floodplain. Wet ground carries a risk of at least `WET_RISK` (0.6), so farmsteads leave it and its fields are meadow. Holdings take up ground once grassed for `grazing_time` (50 years). One at a time, every `reorganise_time` (150 years) on average, a holding moves a threatened seat, is absorbed below 0.3 `holding_area`, takes cheaper cells, or divides above twice `holding_area`. At `BOUNDARY_TIME` (1,000 years) the centreline is kept as a *parish boundary*, which stays put as the channel moves on. Crossing it costs a holding `BOUNDARY_COST` (8 widths) more, so fields settle on it wherever a holding on its far side can reach the ground. Holdings change the river only through their mills.

Holdings take ground only within `WINDOW_MARGIN` (2 widths) of the page's window, `Sim::window`, and the survey reads the ground within `SURVEY_PAD` (8 widths) beyond that. The farmsteads, churches, fishponds, tracks and mills all stand on holdings, so none is founded in the hidden reaches. Jams, tributaries, scroll bars and oxbows still run over the whole river, since they shape the meanders before they come into view. Over 5,000 years with the hidden reaches, seeds 3 and 7 kept 31 and 26 holdings, down from 42 and 45. The median native step fell from 0.164 to 0.146 ms and from 0.174 to 0.156 ms. `Sim::new` leaves the window unbounded, so the tests run as before.

### Farmsteads (`farm.rs`)

Each reorganisation changes a farmstead by one building, towards a plan set by the seat's risk against `SEAT_RISK`. A building past `FLEE_RISK` or off the holding is abandoned first, and ruins fade over `RUIN_TIME` (150 years). Buildings keep `CLEAR` of tributaries, whose courses `Sim` hands over when they change. Each survey removes buildings the water has reached, and queues a new house for any holding left without one, so it rebuilds within 10 years instead of waiting for its next reorganisation. A division makes a hamlet with chance `hamlet_chance`. The farmsteads draw from their own *pseudo-random number generator* (PRNG) stream and spend none of the holdings' search budget, so with `hamlet_chance` 0 the holdings and the river run exactly as with `farmsteads` off.

Once the parish boundary is set, a reorganisation that finds its farmstead's plan complete founds a church with chance `CHURCH_CHANCE` (5%). The seat must stand below `CHURCH_RISK` (0.18), and no standing church may lie within `CHURCH_GAP` (60 widths) of the seat or the site. The churchyard goes on the driest site around the seat, laid east and west. It belongs to no holding, so it stays when the farmsteads move away, and it is abandoned only when its ground passes `FLEE_RISK`. Its ruin fades over `CHURCH_RUIN_TIME` (1,000 years). Seeds 1 and 3 had 4 churches between them after 3,000 years in a valley 112 widths long.

### Fishponds (`ponds.rs`)

A complete farmstead that a church passes over digs a chain of up to `POND_CHAIN` (4) ponds with chance `POND_CHANCE` (half). The chain goes into the clay plug of a filled oxbow within `POND_REACH` (12 widths) of the seat, each pond laid along the old channel, so the chain bends with it, and within the holdings' columns. `Sim` hands the filled oxbows' courses to the holdings at each survey. A pond is abandoned when its holding is gone or its seat moves beyond `POND_KEEP` (16 widths). It silts over `POND_RUIN_TIME` (250 years), and the channel takes any pond it sweeps. Ponds come late, since lakes take centuries to fill. Seeds 1 and 3 had 12 ponds between them after 3,000 years. Churches and ponds draw from a *works* stream of their own. The farmsteads' stream also decides the hamlets, which shape the holdings and through their mills the river, so a draw there would change the river from the first complete farmstead. With the works stream, seed 7 ran identically to the river without them for 5,000 years. Buildings still keep clear of churches and ponds, which can change the farmsteads' draws once one exists.

### Tracks and fords (`tracks.rs`)

Tracks join the farmsteads and cross the channel only at fords, since no bridge would stand for long. One search goes out from every farmstead at once, `TRACK_BUDGET` (128) cells a step, costed by flood risk and by `FORD_COST` for each width of channel waded. Its parts meet in 8 bands of rows, one a step. The cheapest meetings are laid first, as a *greedy spanner*: a lane goes in only if the network already laid makes the trip more than `DETOUR` (2) times as long. Ground on the last network costs `TRACK_KEEP` (half), so ways stay in use. A network takes about 340 steps (85 years). The fords are found again on the tracks after every survey of the channel, so they follow the water. Standing churches are searched from as farmsteads are, so the network gathers on them. The search stops at the holdings' columns.

Each cell keeps the time it has carried a track. A lane is a full *hollow way* after `HOLLOW_TIME` (1,000 years). When the network leaves a lane worn for more than `HOLLOW_MIN` (100 years), the lane stays as an earthwork that fills at `HOLLOW_FILL` (a quarter) of the rate it wore, until the channel sweeps it. Each laying of the network adds the time since the last to the cells of the last network, and splits the abandoned ways at the cells it takes up again. After every survey, the cells the water has taken lose their wear. Tracks read the land and change nothing else.

### Floodplain processes

- Clearance: `Sim::supply()` rises from 1 to `clearance_supply` from `clearance_start` (1,500 years) over `clearance_time` (1,000 years). It multiplies overbank silt, lake filling and fan growth.
- Wood (`wood.rs`): mature forest on eroding banks drops jams. A jam pushes the flow off its bank and breaks up over `jam_lifetime`, or is lost beyond `JAM_HOLD` of the centreline.
- Tributaries (`tributary.rs`): combes 3 widths deep or more carry a stream. It builds a gravel fan into the `coarse` grid (`coarse_factor`) and speeds migration below its confluence. Its course is re-cut every 10 steps at the first water, so it changes only at its lower end. It shifts on its fan at `stream_shift_rate` (once per 1,000 years) and builds a delta into an open lake at `delta_rate`.
- Mills (`mills.rs`): holdings found mills on straight reaches. A *weir* holds its reach and ponds the reach above until the channel outflanks or leaves it. A *leat*'s ends follow the bank, and the mill is ruined when the bank reaches its course.

### Hydraulics and PRNG streams

`ips.rs` derives Ω, Γ and α from hydraulic inputs, and `rng.rs` is the local seeded PRNG. Falling wood, new mills and scroll bars draw from `events`, tributary shifts from `streams`, and walls, farmsteads, and churches with fishponds from streams of their own. Turning a process off leaves the river's own draws unchanged.

## meander-wasm (`wasm/`)

`River` wraps `Sim` with hidden reaches of `UPSTREAM` = 60 and `DOWNSTREAM` = 20 widths. `sync()` copies geometry into flat `f32` buffers exposed as raw pointers. `sync_scrolls`, `sync_plugs` (time since cutoff per oxbow cell) and `sync_forest` (growth per cell) can also grow WebAssembly (WASM) memory, so the worker's `pack` calls them all before it reads the memory's buffer. `sync_line` copies only the centreline and widths, for the warm-up's point-bar capture. `soil_ptr` points at the erodibility noise, fixed at reset, and `param_names` lists the parameters. `jams`, `buildings`, `ponds`, `tributaries` and `mills` return `Vec<f32>` copies, which also allocate, as do `track_depths` and the abandoned hollow ways' `hollow_points`, `hollow_lengths` and `hollow_depths`. `coarse_ptr` points at the gravel grid, and `mill_version`, `building_version` and `wall_version` tell the renderer when to rebuild.

## web (`web/`)

The river steps in a *dedicated worker* (`worker.js`), a thread of its own, while the page draws. After each request the worker packs everything the renderer reads into one `ArrayBuffer` and transfers it without copying (`pack` in `snapshot.js`). On the page, `RiverView` answers the same calls as `River` from that snapshot. Each `*_ptr` is a byte offset into the snapshot's buffer, and `memory.buffer` is the buffer itself, so the renderer builds its views as it would on WASM memory. A renderer read of a new `River` accessor needs an entry in `snapshot.js`. `soil` reads the noise grid sent at each reset, and `wall_foot` reads samples of the walls' feet along the window, sent when `wall_version` changes. A snapshot is 1.2 MB for a desktop window 768 units wide and 1.4 MB for a phone's 1,039, mostly seven field grids. Packing one takes 0.2 ms in Node (seed 7, after 1,000 units).

`ui.js` owns the panel, the frame loop and the warm-up. It keeps one stepping request in flight and draws the latest snapshot. A reset drops the replies to earlier requests. The warm-up runs in requests of `WARM_BUDGET` (12 ms), and the worker returns the centreline after every 50 steps for the point bars. The panel shows the speed slider, the tick (`River::steps` since the reset, warm-up included) and JavaScript frame-time percentiles.

A replay restarts the river, runs it to a tick and pauses. The Link's `view=W` is the window width in world units, because the river's bounds follow the window and a slightly wider one soon diverges. The warm-up and replays record the centreline every 50 steps from the reset, so two replays of one link give identical Scalable Vector Graphics (SVG). A live run records each frame, so its point bars differ. `ui.js` sets `data-idle` once a paused page has nothing left to draw. The loop draws only after a step or a setting change, and schedules no frames while paused with no floodplain redraw under way. The channel is the exception: while the river runs, it trails the river by one step, and `tween` redraws it between the last two centrelines. Each point of the newer line moves out from its foot on the older one, and a cutoff makes the channel jump across. At the default speed the river steps every third frame by up to a pixel, so the channel would otherwise move at 20 Hz; trailing it costs about 1.2 ms a frame. The feet are found once a pair, with the furthest any point or bank in the window moves between the two. A frame redraws the channel only once it could have moved `MIN_MOVE` (0.5) device pixels since the last redraw. At the default speed it moves 0.1–0.3 CSS pixels a frame on an 844 × 390 phone, so on a phone with 3 device pixels to the CSS pixel 177 of 301 frames redraw it. At 8× throttling there, the mean frame fell from 14.1–14.2 ms to 8.5–8.9 ms over three alternating 10 s runs. The channel is drawn at `river.time() + owed - dt`. A reply moves time from the time owed to the river's time, so the two sum to an even clock, and while the worker steps the channel waits at the newest snapshot. Subtracting the time asked as well sent the channel back to the start of its step at each request, 201 times in 602 frames in Chrome. At the default speed it then moved only as each step came in, and 200 of 602 frames redrew it. In Safari, where the reply often comes a frame later, the drawn time went back 91 times in 508 frames. It now never goes back, and in headless Chrome (1280 × 800 at 2×, default speed) the channel redraws in 362 of 602 frames, as `MIN_MOVE` allows. The device pixel scale is read once per resize, since reading the window's size after the frame's writes forced a layout, which raised `tween` at speed 60 from 9.4 to 12.8 ms a frame.

`main.js` draws eight stacked SVGs: `#bars` (old point bars), `#band` (the growing point-bar band, `barLayer`), `#ground` (coarse ground, forest, upland), `#fields` (the field layer and its clip paths), `#lower` (scars, scarps, scroll bars), `#swept` (swept ground, `sweptLayer`), `#upper` (levees, marsh, ponds, tracks, farmsteads, crowns, boundary) and `#view` (channel and lakes). All but `#view` are *compositing layers* (`will-change: transform`), so a moving channel does not repaint them. The renderer's constructor takes `[band, ground, fields, lower, swept, upper]`, and `save` copies every layer. The fields got a layer of their own because of the `#farmland` *mask*, eight nested grey regions that faded them in as the sward thickened and a black region that kept them off the hanger woods. Safari renders a mask again whenever anything in its layer repaints, and it paints on the page's thread. Chrome rasterises on other threads, so the cost did not show there. With the fields among the rest of the floodplain, every floodplain redraw re-rendered all the fields through the mask. At the default speed the floodplain layers finished 86 redraws in 20 s: scroll bars 39, farmsteads 11, fields 8 and crowns 8. With the split, only field, forest and wall redraws repainted `#fields`, 14 of the 86, since the forest rewrote the mask's fade regions and the walls set the hanger-wood region. In Safari on an Apple M1 laptop (1440 × 666 window, 2 device px to the CSS px), seed 7 at the default speed drew 464–537 of 600 frames before the split, with 46–56 gaps over 30 ms, and 570 frames with 9 gaps after it. Frames were counted by `requestAnimationFrame` for 10 s after a 2 s settle. Removing the mask gave 600 frames and 3 gaps, hiding the floodplain 602 and 1, and flat pebble and reed patterns 498 and 57, no gain. The split changes no colour by more than 3 levels of 255, except a handful of pixels where separate layers blend. Each field, forest or wall redraw still re-rendered the mask, and each held up a frame by 30–100 ms. *Clip paths* now take the mask's place (see Floodplain), since WebKit applies a clip path that holds one shape as geometry, with no offscreen image. Over 10 s on the same window, seed and speed, the masked split-layer build showed 569 frames, with 15 gaps over 30 ms and the longest 89–108 ms. Without the mask it showed 588 frames, with 10 gaps over 30 ms and the longest 51–57 ms. Against the masked build, 91 pixels of the desktop render differ by more than 16 levels, and the stacked opacities differ by at most 9 of 255. The growing band and the swept ground then got layers of their own, since Safari repaints whatever lies under a change in its layer, on the page's thread. Both change about 1.5 times a second. Each kept centreline adds a path, and their tones shift through `--tone` custom properties in a `style` attribute. The band lay in `#ground` over the forest, and the swept ground in `#upper` over the crowns, so each such frame repainted the forest or the crowns beneath. The scars, scarps and scroll bars under the swept ground moved to `#lower`, which keeps the stacking order. A per-frame log of *Document Object Model* (DOM) mutations in Safari, on the same window, seed and speed over 10 s, gave the gap after each frame. Frames that added a band path went from 15 to 19, their gaps over 30 ms from 6 to 0, and their median gap from 30 to 17 ms. Frames that added a swept-ground path went from 17 to 21, from 7 gaps over 30 ms to 0, and from a median of 30 to 17 ms. Frames that changed only tones went from 24 to 20, from 5 gaps to 1, and from 20 to 17 ms. Frames with nothing changed kept a median gap of 17 ms. The fade clips now wait for the field redraw (see Floodplain), so frames that swapped them fell from 8, 7 of them over 30 ms, to 4, each alongside a field redraw. Over the run the page drew 593 frames, with 10 gaps over 30 ms and 1 over 45 ms, against 585, 17 and 3. Renders against the build before differ by at most 8 of 255, where separate layers blend. Field redraws leave the longest gaps. All 4 left gaps over 30 ms, with a median of 39.5 ms, and one of 74 ms followed a frame with 29 ms of JavaScript. Each replaces about 200 of 650 field paths, and the replaced shapes change, with about half of each outline's vertices moved. Crowns and farmsteads share `#upper`. Crown redraws left 3 gaps over 30 ms in 6 frames, with a median of 31.5 ms, and farmstead redraws 4 in 7, with a median of 31 ms. Floodplain layers share `FLOODPLAIN_BUDGET` (4 ms) a frame, or `FLOODPLAIN_IDLE_BUDGET` (10 ms) on an otherwise idle frame, oldest picture first. When a redraw has been due for longer than the lag limit, `ui.js` holds the river until the floodplain catches up. The limit is `MAX_LAG` (25 years) or `LAG_SECONDS` (0.5 s) at the set speed, whichever is longer. With the limit in simulated time alone, a redraw spread over 10 frames at full speed held the river twice a second. Before the hold, the river slows to the speed at which every redraw under way would finish inside the limit, from a running mean of each layer's redraw time in real time, scaled by 1 / `PACE_LAG` (1). A margin of 1 / 0.7 slowed the river by 1–11% on a phone at 4× throttling, and neither stood still for 3 frames. A redraw's time counts at most `REDRAW_GAP` (100 ms) a frame, so a redraw due while the tab was hidden does not slow the river after it. A redraw takes about as long in real time at any speed, so on a slow machine the river settles at a steady lower speed. It slows at once and speeds up over `PACE_SECONDS` (1 s), though a new speed from the slider takes effect at once while the floodplain is not holding the river back. The floodplain's budget rises towards the idle budget only as a redraw passes half the lag. Giving it the idle budget whenever the floodplain set the speed ran the river 17% faster on the 8× phone at speed 60 (12.5 against 10.7 units/s), but cut the frames that showed it moving from 28 to 24 a second. A redraw *episode* ends when its layer shows a new picture, even if the next is due at once; counting from the first due frame instead let the lag grow across back-to-back redraws. After a layer shows a new picture it *rests* for `REDRAW_REST` (0.5 s real time) before it may start another, unless its redraw is under way or the river is settling. Its refreshes are set in simulated time, so without the rest the forest falls due six times a second at full speed. A resting layer does not count as due (`rested`, inside each layer's `due`), so the rest does not slow the river. Against the same build with `REDRAW_REST` 0 (seed 7, 1280 × 800, speed 60, two alternating 10 s runs after the warm-up), the river ran 8,019–8,100 steps in 10 s at 4× throttling, against 4,817–4,852. Unthrottled, a step took 406–410 µs of main-thread time, against 559–563. On Chrome at 8× throttling on an 844 × 390 viewport, seed 7 from tick 10,000 at speed 60, the river held in 251 of 377 frames over 10 s with the hold alone. With the pacing it moved in every one of 256 frames, at 13.6 units a second (34 years a second). A desktop at speed 60 steps in every frame, at 58 units a second. A stepping request may take `STEP_SHARE` (80%) of the last frame's interval, at least `STEP_BUDGET` (8 ms), and `ui.js` drops the simulated time left over, and any owed beyond the request under way, one step and `MAX_OWED` (40 ms at the river's speed) while a reply is late, so the river does not lurch on when it comes. Without that, a machine too slow for the set speed owes each frame the steps of the last, longer one: on a phone-class CPU (Chrome at 8× throttling) at full speed, frames reached 200 ms when the river stepped on the page's thread. Lake rebuilds share `LAKE_BUDGET` (2 ms) a draw, in turn, because at full speed every lake's fill passes a `LAKE_STEP` between draws. A lake the channel has reshaped rebuilds at once. On a pause or a single step, `settle` finishes every redraw in one frame, and the draw after it rebuilds every waiting lake. The end of the warm-up sets `settle` too, so the first frame shows the floodplain whole. Before, the first frames showed crowns over every field until the field redraw finished a few frames later.

Path data is written in relative coordinates from a pen held in whole tenths of a unit (`penAt`, `penTo`, `penOff`, `num`, `pair`, `sep`). Each point lands where `dp1` would put it, in fewer characters. Every subpath starts with an absolute `M`, so data from separate calls can be joined. Valley walls, scars, lakes, contours, bars (`cropped`) and polylines all go through `lineData` and `ringData`. The channel's edges (`edgeData`) write relative `l` and `c` commands, with a command letter only where it changes. A circle (`circleData`) is two relative arcs, with their flags written without spaces. `lineData` leaves out each point within `SIMPLIFY` (0.15 units) of the line through its kept neighbours, by *Douglas–Peucker simplification* (`keepPoints`, in scratch buffers, with no allocation). That is about 0.25 px in a 1280 px desktop window 768 units wide, and 0.37 device px on a landscape phone at 3 device px to the CSS px. Channel edges with curves are not simplified. A static frame on seed 7 at tick 4,000 (1280 × 800) holds 539 KB of path data in 1,410 elements, against 1,792 KB in 822 elements with absolute coordinates. Relative coordinates and the compact circles alone gave 1,410 KB. Seed 3 at tick 14,000 (844 × 390) holds 643 KB in 1,894 elements, against 1,957 KB in 1,251.

Every floodplain redraw re-rasterises all of its own layer. The figures below were measured before the floodplain was split into layers, when everything above the old point bars was one `#ground` layer. In Chrome on an Apple M1 (headless, with GPU rasterisation), each layer was redrawn in full 16 times on a frozen replay (seed 7, tick 4,000, 1280 × 800, two runs). The main thread of the graphics processing unit (GPU) process spent 4.5–4.8 ms on a forest or field redraw. With absolute coordinates it spent 8.2–8.5 ms, and with relative data and crown dots but no simplification 6.1–6.3 ms. A wall redraw fell from 9.6–9.8 ms to 6.3–6.7 ms. A forest redraw's page-thread time stayed at about 12.9 ms. JavaScript rose from 10.4–10.6 to 10.8–11.0 ms and paint from 0.4 to 0.85 ms, while style fell from 1.0 to 0.84 ms and garbage collection from 0.85–1.06 to 0.27 ms. `setAttribute` takes only 0.1–0.7 ms a redraw, so the bytes cost little to parse, and fewer calls save more than fewer bytes.

### Channel and lakes

The channel is a filled outline at the local width, drawn in runs of `RUN` (96) segments. Only points within `CHANNEL_MARGIN` of the window are drawn, though curvature and widths span the whole river. The last run stops at the end of the shown span, so no run reaches beyond the window. Against runs of 24, runs of 96 cut a channel redraw's page-thread time by about 0.4 ms (seed 7, 1280 × 800) and left its GPU time unchanged. One path for the whole channel cut 0.5 ms but added 0.3 ms of GPU time. Lakes, oxbow scars, streams, jams, fords, the parish boundary and soil patches are drawn only where they reach the window, and mills only where their leat or pond could. At tick 14,000, the paths lying wholly outside the window fell from 25 to 1 on seed 3 and from 53 to 3 on seed 1. Pools, *shoals*, riffles and mid-channel bars come from the smoothed curvature and width. Pools lie against the outer bank of a bend, and shoals of shallow water against the inner bank, which with the riffles carry the shallows from the inner bank of one bend to the next. A mid-channel bar has a blunt head and a tail that narrows downstream, lies towards the inner bank in a bend, and rises from shallow water through wet sand to dry sand. The depth bands share one path per colour, which cut a further 0.2 ms of style and paint from a channel redraw. All of them redraw with the banks: riffles and bars redrawn one draw in three visibly lagged the channel. The work is limited to the points in the window, and bands from the bank stop `THIN_BAND` (0.03) widths short of their full reach, so they are not drawn as slivers between bends. On seed 7 at 4× throttling in an 844 × 390 window, the median channel redraw rose from 3.2–3.4 ms to 4.5–4.6 ms over two 10 s runs each. A lake is drawn on its bed, a parabola across the old channel (`lakeBed`). Reeds fill the water shallower than the open water's edge, so they reach furthest over a gently sloping bed and grow out over each plug. Every lake's reeds lie under the water of all lakes, so lakes that meet read as one. Over the water lie two deeper bands, set towards the old outer bank by the bend's curvature; silt plumes that fade along the lake from each end, and around the head of a delta, stronger as clearance raises the supply; water lilies on the shallows from about 750 years after the cutoff; and duckweed mats late in the fill. Lilies and duckweed are seeded by their place along the whole oxbow, as the scar's tufts are. `tributaries` marks a stream that ends in a lake, for its delta's plume.

### Floodplain

Fields are strips along each holding's long axis. Arable fields carry *ridge and furrow*: furrows `FURROW_GAP` (0.6 widths) apart along the field, each bent in the *reversed S* an ox team left by swinging left before the headland, `FURROW_S` (5%) of the field's length either way, and stopping short of each end. The furrows are clipped to the field's own cells and fade in with the fields. They added 4–8 ms to a field redraw of 54–58 ms at 4× throttling (seed 3 from tick 14,000, seven redraws each way). Fields redraw after a reorganisation, at most every `FIELD_REFRESH`, and write the page only when done, swapping in twins of the paths that changed. The fields fade in from `grazing_time` to `vegetation_time` (50 to 250 years), so they follow the channel onto new bars. The forest redraw contours its blurred age grid into `FIELD_FADE` (8) nested regions between those times, and leaves them in `fadeNext` without putting them on the page. Each region is a clip path (`fade-1` to `fade-8`) of one even-odd path. A field the fade has not fully reached is drawn as several copies. One unclipped copy takes the opacity of the lowest region the field lies wholly in, and one copy is clipped to each higher region it reaches. `fadeStep` sets each copy's opacity so that k stacked copies give k/8 of the field's opacity. A field wholly faded in is drawn once. The furrows and hedges of fading fields lie in one path in `<defs>`, drawn by 8 `<use>` elements, each clipped to one region. Those of fields wholly faded in are a plain path. A clip on the whole field layer (`#unwooded`) keeps the fields off the hanger woods: one huge square (`OUTSIDE`) with each wood cut out as an even-odd hole, which the wall redraw leaves in `unwoodedNext`. A field redraw takes both at its start and puts them on the page in its last stage, with the fields drawn for them, so `#fields` repaints once per forest cycle, down from twice. Which copies a field needs depends on the forest and the walls, so a forest or wall redraw sets `fieldsStale`, and the fields fall due. The field redraw reads the forest's blurred age grid (`treeBlur`). Forest growth is contoured into shrub and forest, a contour a stage, and sizes the tree crowns, a `TREE_STRIP` at a time; a crop mask (`cropMask`) clears crowns from the fields' cells. The crowns go stale (`treesStale`) only when those cells change, so a field redraw that only moves the fade leaves them alone. Before, a wall redraw after the last field redraw left the crop cells out of date. Each circle of a crown is a *dot*, a zero-length line with round caps stroked at its diameter, in about 12 characters of path data where two arcs took about 46. On seed 7 at tick 4,000 the crowns hold 110 KB of path data, against 483 KB as arcs. A path has one stroke width, so radii are rounded to classes `CROWN_CLASS` (2^(1/4)) apart, within 9%, from `CROWN_MIN` (0.025 widths). The class comes from a table indexed by the radius in tenths. Each strip has one path per kind and class, in a group per class that sets the stroke width. Each kind's opacity is set on its group, so its dots do not build up where classes or strips overlap. Classes 2^(1/6) apart gave 840 crown paths and about 0.7 ms more paint a forest redraw; at 2^(1/4) the forest redraw's page-thread time matches that of the arc crowns. Each stage sets its path data on detached twins of the paths, since Chrome parses path data when it is set, and the last stage swaps the twins onto the page. A finished forest redraw drops any crown redraw under way, which would use stale growth. Farmsteads lie under the crowns and redraw on `building_version`, at least every 50 years. Wet ground is blurred twice and contoured into marsh with the survey maps' reed sign, every `COARSE_REFRESH` (50 years). Tracks lie under the farmsteads and redraw when they change; a cell that several tracks share is drawn once. Each of `HOLLOW_LEVELS` of depth adds a pair of banks under the stretches deeper than it, a shaded bank set towards the upper-left light and a lit bank away from it, so a lane's banks stack as it sinks. An abandoned hollow way keeps its banks over a grassy floor. A church is a west tower, a nave and a chancel in an oval walled churchyard with a yew or two, and crowns stand clear of the churchyard. Fishponds lie over the plug marsh and under the tracks, with spoil banks and a reed fringe, and an abandoned pond turns to marsh as it fades. The parish boundary is drawn once, as a dash-dot line over the crowns and under the water.

### Valley walls, streams, jams and mills

The walls are drawn from `wall_foot` and `wall_combes` without the pinches, which only keep bends off the window edges. They redraw after a slump, at most every 50 years, in budgeted stages, and the page and the wall lines the fields read change only in the last stage. The `#unwooded` clip waits for the next field redraw. Each scarp is shaded in nested bands, darker on the top wall, which faces away from the upper-left light, and lighter on the bottom wall. Its hachures are wedges that thin down the slope, as on old survey maps. Tributaries redraw when a course changes. Leats lie under the water, and fords, jams and mills over it. A working weir follows the channel, and its leat redraws each frame to the bank.
