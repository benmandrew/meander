# CLAUDE.md

This file provides guidance to Claude Code (claude.ai/code) when working with code in this repository.

A live browser simulation of a meandering river: a Rust core compiled to WASM, drawn as SVG. The panel shows only a speed slider; the simulation runs on its `Params` defaults.

The simulation runs over years, not weeks. One unit of simulated time is 2.5 years (`YEARS_PER_UNIT` in `ui.js`), the warm-up alone is about 1,250 years, and forest takes about 3,400 years to mature. Describe every change, in code comments and in prose, on that timescale.

## Commands

The Nix flake supplies every tool below. `.envrc` loads its dev shell through direnv (run `direnv allow` once), or enter it with `nix develop`. The shell's `wasm-bindgen-cli` version is read from `Cargo.lock`, so after bumping the `wasm-bindgen` crate, nixpkgs must carry the matching `wasm-bindgen-cli_0_2_N`.

- `nix build .#web-dist`: build the deployable site (HTML, JS, CSS and `pkg/`) into `result/`, without `wasm-pack`.
- `make build`: build the WASM package into `web/pkg` (needs `wasm-pack`).
- `make serve`: build, then serve `web/` at http://localhost:8000. `?warmup=T` overrides the warm-up in simulated time.
- `make test`: `cargo test --release -p meander-core`. Tests run long simulations, so always use `--release`.
- One test: `cargo test --release -p meander-core <test_name>`, e.g. `sine_perturbation_grows_and_drifts_downstream`.
- Snapshots for tuning: `cargo run --release -p meander-core --example snapshot -- <out_dir> [steps] [every] [seed]`. The `reach` column is the mean sinuosity of the reaches that set the channel slope. Override any parameter with its upper-case name as an env var (`ALPHA=0.8`). `UP` and `DOWN` set hidden reach lengths in widths.

## Architecture

Three layers, with data flowing one way:

- `meander-core` is pure Rust with no dependencies. `Sim::step` resamples the centreline, computes Menger curvature, migrates points along their normals using the Howard–Knutson upstream-weighted kernel (an O(n) forward recurrence), smooths, clamps to the domain, then runs the hard neck cutoff and any flood-driven cutoffs. It resamples again after every cut. Flow runs from index 0 (upstream) to the last index. Geometry is `f64`. The rate is scaled by the channel slope (`Sim::slope`): the valley slope divided by the sinuosity of the reach `SLOPE_REACH` (12) widths either side, raised to `slope_exponent` (0.5, Chézy velocity) and taken relative to a reach of sinuosity `slope_sinuosity` (1.25, the mean measured without the feedback). A winding reach migrates more slowly, so necks close later and floods make a larger share of the cutoffs. `field.rs` holds the fBm erodibility grid, at half a width per cell. Each step stamps the cells under the channel as fresh point-bar sand with no vegetation growth. Growth, in age-equivalent time, decides when shrub binds the sand (at `vegetation_time`) and when forest stiffens it to the old floodplain's erodibility (a further `forest_time`). Each step advances one of 10 bands of rows, so every cell grows every 10 steps, at a rate set by the cell's overbank deposit, from 0.6 on bare bar sand to 1.6 at `vegetation_height`, so a median bar binds in about `vegetation_time`. Floods strip shrub still younger than `vegetation_time` within `scour_reach` of the bank. A cutoff stamps the oxbow footprint as a lake that fills to a clay plug. Floods lay overbank deposits on it, which slow erosion where banks are high and are drawn as levee contours. Migration reads the grid and the valley-wall taper at the bank a point moves into, chosen by the sign of the rate alone, not at the centreline. `walls.rs` sets the valley walls at each reset, from a stream of the seed of their own so the river's draws are unchanged: each wall's foot wanders up to 2.5 widths outwards of `valley_width`, never inwards, and combes cut back into it up to 4.5 widths. `Sim::floor` gives the floor between the feet; the taper reads it narrowed at the pinches, the land survey without them, as drawn. Each step also cuts an oxbow where the channel centreline crosses one of its segments or comes within `REWORK_RADIUS` (0.1 widths) of a point, so a channel alongside a lake hides it under the water before removing it. A new oxbow absorbs the parts of older oxbows under its water, leaving each remnant one point inside it so the lakes join. `Sim::capture` lets the channel take up an open lake (fill below `CAPTURE_FILL`) whose banks meet the channel's at two places, when the channel between them is longer than the route through the lake by more than `capture_ratio`; the lake becomes channel and the bypassed reach a new oxbow. The lake's ends are plugged for `END_PLUG` widths, and a route that turns through a right angle or crosses the channel is rejected. Lake silt is read only from cells still stamped as lake; each lake fills over `oxbow_lifetime`, faster where floods lay silt in it or bedload enters a connected entrance, then stays for `scar_lifetime` as a scar. Floods also leave scroll bars along inner banks (`Sim::scrolls`), whose ridges raise the ground by `scroll_height` so vegetation takes to them first. Each point carries a local width (`Sim::widths`, in multiples of `width`), after the bank-pull and bar-push model of Parker et al. (2011): the outer bank retreats at the migration rate times its erodibility, the inner bank advances at `bar_push` times the rate, and the width relaxes to 1 over `width_time`. Floods widen bends by `flood_widening`. The centreline keeps the Howard–Knutson rate, so width changes the planform only through the bank reads and the cutoffs, which fire when the banks meet. Each oxbow keeps the widths of the channel it was cut from, for its footprint, its bedload entrance and its drawn lake and scar. `land.rs` shares the ground between medieval landholdings, each the geodesic Voronoi cell of a farmstead over the grid: the channel and oxbow ground are barriers, and flood risk (falling with distance from the water) raises the cost of crossing a cell. Holdings take up ground beside them once grass has grown on it for `grazing_time` (20 units, 50 years) and keep ground the river cuts them off from. Each reorganises at random, with mean interval `reorganise_time` (60 units, 150 years), one at a time: it moves a threatened farmstead, is absorbed below 0.3 `holding_area`, takes cells it reaches more cheaply than their holder by a margin, and divides above twice `holding_area`. Holdings read the river and never change it. `ips.rs` derives Ω, Γ and α from hydraulic inputs. The neck search and the oxbow and scroll rework share one hashed grid, rebuilt only when `line_version` shows the centreline has moved. `oxbow_version` counts changes to oxbow shapes, not fills, so `sync` can skip recomputing their heights. `rng.rs` is a local seeded PRNG; do not add `rand` or `getrandom`.
- `meander-wasm` wraps `Sim` as `River`. The simulated river is longer than the visible window: hidden reaches of `UPSTREAM` = 60 and `DOWNSTREAM` = 20 widths let bends develop before they come into view. `sync()` copies geometry into flat `f32` buffers exposed as raw pointers. JS must call `sync()` after stepping and rebuild every typed-array view, because WASM memory growth detaches old views. `sync_scrolls`, `sync_plugs` and `sync_forest` can grow memory too, so `draw` runs the floodplain layers that call them before it builds its own views. `sync_forest` copies the vegetation growth of each cell, which `main.js` contours into nested shrub and forest regions, each fading in over several contours, and uses to size seeded tree crowns on young and mature forest. `sync_plugs` copies the cells still stamped as oxbow, which `main.js` draws as faint marsh after the lake and scar are gone. `sync_line` copies only the centreline and its widths, for the warm-up's point-bar capture.
- `web/` is plain ES modules with no bundler. `ui.js` owns the control panel, the frame loop and the warm-up (spread across frames). `main.js` draws the SVG, including seeded soil patches as vector blobs typed by the erodibility noise. Every lake's reed fringe is stroked in a layer under the water of all lakes, so lakes that meet read as one body of water. The channel is a filled outline at the local width in runs of 24 segments, overlapping by a segment so no seam shows, with the inner bank clamped short of the centre of tight bends. Pools, riffles and mid-channel bars are drawn over the water from the curvature and local width, both smoothed along the river. It draws into three stacked SVGs: `#bars` holds the old point bars, `#ground` the floodplain, and `#view` the channel and lakes. The first two are compositing layers, so a moving channel does not repaint them. Fields are laid out in strips along each holding's long axis from its seed, traced along the cell edges of the label map into arcs shared by neighbouring fields, simplified and rounded, and redrawn after a reorganisation finishes, at most every `FIELD_REFRESH`. Meadow takes ground at high flood risk and arable the drier ground. Fields are masked by nested contours that fade them in from `grazing_time` (50 years), where holdings take up grassed ground, to `vegetation_time` (250 years), so the channel cuts them as it migrates and they follow it onto the bars it leaves. Tree crowns in crop fields are skipped on farmland, by a per-cell crop mask; crowns in woods and beside hedges stay. The valley walls are drawn from the core's feet (`wall_foot`) and combes (`wall_combes`), without the pinches, which exist only to keep bends off the window edges. The upland's tint lies under the fields. Hachures, shaded for light from the upper left, run down each scarp over the fields, as on old survey maps, and hanger woods on the steeper stretches and in combes hide the fields and let tree crowns stand. The frame loop draws only when a step was taken or a setting changed, and `set` skips attribute writes whose value has not changed. The frontend is SVG only. A WebGL view existed in commit e6cd034 and was removed at the user's request; do not reintroduce it without asking. Likewise a raster floodplain map (canvas or embedded image) was tried and rejected; keep floodplain detail as vector shapes.

### Parameters are addressed by string name

Adding a simulation parameter touches three places: the `Params` field and its `Default`, `Params::NAMES`, the `Params::field` match (or a special case in `get`/`set` for non-`f64` values such as `cutoff_min_sep` and `physics`). The snapshot example picks up env overrides from `NAMES` with no further change. Most length parameters are in channel widths, not world units.

With the `physics` flag on, every `Params::set` call re-derives `omega`, `gamma` and `alpha` from `friction`, `aspect`, `froude` and `scour`, so setting those three directly has no effect. The flag is on by default, with hydraulics of a lowland sand-bed river.

## Constraints

- Step time must stay even. Periodic work over the grid is spread across steps in bands of rows (vegetation growth, the land survey over `SURVEY_STEPS`), and searches carry on across steps within a per-step `BUDGET`. Check with the snapshot example's step-time percentiles.
- The hot path avoids `hypot`, `ln` and `cos`. They are software routines in WASM and made a step 3.6× slower than native. `Sim` keeps scratch buffers to avoid per-step allocation.
- The validation tests in `src/lib.rs` pin the model's behaviour. A sine perturbation must grow and drift downstream (this checks the sign convention). A local-only kernel must not drift. Noise runs must cut off, never self-intersect, and keep sinuosity bounded. Run `make test` after any change to the dynamics.
- Commits use conventional prefixes, e.g. `feat: stochastic flood-driven cutoffs`.
